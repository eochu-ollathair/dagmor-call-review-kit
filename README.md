# Four calls, four different review questions

This free practice sheet uses invented conversations. Read each short extract, decide what the review should record, then compare your answer with the notes below. None of these are customer calls.

## 1. Was a callback actually agreed?

> **Customer:** Could you ring me tomorrow around lunch?
>
> **Agent:** Yes, I'll sort that out.
>
> **Customer:** Great, thanks.

**Your call:** Would you mark a booked callback? What would you check before deciding?

## 2. Who earned the praise?

> **Customer:** Nine out of ten for the driver. He carried the boxes in when he saw my bad back. But accounts still haven't returned the money you billed twice.

**Your call:** What should the praise go to, and what problem is still open?

## 3. Did the required step happen?

> **Agent:** Before we finish, I need to go through one more point—
>
> **Recording ends.**

**Your call:** Can you mark that point as covered from this recording?

## 4. Was the objection handled well?

> **Customer:** That sounds dearer than what I use now.
>
> **Agent:** Fair point. Which part of your current bill are you comparing it with? I can explain the difference, and you can decide whether it is worth changing.
>
> **Customer:** Send me the details. I haven't decided.

**Your call:** What did the agent do well, and what outcome can you actually record?

## Answer notes

1. **Callback:** The words show an offer and a general time, not a confirmed booking. Check the separate booking record or the rest of the original call before marking an agreed time.
2. **Praise and problem:** The driver earned the praise. The double billing and missing refund remain open. Keeping only the nine out of ten would lose that distinction.
3. **Coverage:** The saved audio ends before the step is heard. Mark the recording as incomplete or the step as unverified, then check whether the customer's full original recording exists. Do not turn an unheard step into a failure or a pass.
4. **Objection:** The agent acknowledged the concern and asked a useful question. The customer requested details but did not agree to buy. Credit the handling where your chosen script allows it; record the actual outcome separately.

For a real review, keep the recording filename, the words that support each finding, the chosen script, and any human correction together. A number alone cannot tell the whole story.
